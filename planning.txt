# Project: Market Signal AI

Repository name:

`market-signal-ai`

Build a clean MVP web application named **Market Signal AI**.

Market Signal AI is a multi-AI investment signal dashboard.

The core concept is:

> Users add investment products they want to track. Multiple AI analysts independently analyze each product and provide simple actionable signals such as ADD, HOLD, REDUCE, or EXIT.

The application should NOT look like a traditional brokerage platform.

The main product experience is:

**Tracked Product × AI Signal**

The home page exists primarily to answer:

> What are my AI analysts currently recommending for the products I follow?

Detailed portfolio information, market data, reasoning, Root Events, and technical analysis belong on the product detail page, not the home page.

# 3. Product Philosophy

The UI should be extremely focused.

- a brokerage application
- a TradingView clone
- a portfolio accounting system
- a Bloomberg-style terminal

It is primarily:

→ View tracked products

→ Scan AI signals

→ Notice an interesting or changed signal

# 20. AI Analysis Framework

AI providers should not simply receive a vague prompt such as:

"You are a top stock analyst. Tell me whether I should buy or sell."

Each AI analysis should consider:

1. Root Event
2. Market Regime
3. Trend
4. Momentum
5. Volume / Price
6. Institutional / Fund Flow data when available
7. Current Position
8. Risk / Reward
9. Previous AI Decision
10. Invalidation Conditions

The final output must choose exactly one:

ADD

HOLD

REDUCE

EXIT

ADD and REDUCE may include quantity.

Example:

# 21. Root Event

Root Event means:

The primary underlying event, narrative, macro factor, earnings development, policy development, or market catalyst currently driving the investment thesis.

The AI should determine:

- What is driving the market/product?
- Is it confirmed or expected?
- Is it bullish, bearish, or neutral?
- Is it still active?
- Has it potentially been priced in?
- What would invalidate it?

The AI must distinguish between:

confirmed facts

and

market expectations.

Do not allow the model to invent Root Events when supporting data is unavailable.

# 27. Decision Rules

The AI decision engine should follow these principles:

1. Do not sell solely because the position is profitable.

2. Allow profitable positions to continue when the thesis and trend remain valid.

3. Allow partial reduction when risk/reward deteriorates.

4. Exit when the underlying thesis or defined invalidation condition fails.

5. Do not average down solely because price fell.

6. ADD requires new positive evidence.

7. Position size must be considered when position information exists.

8. Previous AI decisions should be considered to reduce unnecessary back-and-forth trading.

9. Do not attempt to perfectly predict tops or bottoms.

10. If evidence is insufficient, clearly express lower confidence.

---

# 28. Bull Case / Bear Case

Before making the final decision, require the analysis to consider both sides.

Bull Case:

Why should the user continue holding or add?

Bear Case:

Why should the user reduce or exit?

The final decision should be based on the relative evidence rather than prompt wording.

This is intended to reduce confirmation bias.

---

# 29. Invalidation

Every AI recommendation should answer:

> What would make this analysis wrong?

Example:

Current recommendation:

HOLD

Invalidation:

Major support breaks with increasing volume while the primary bullish Root Event also weakens.

The invalidation condition should be stored with the analysis result.
